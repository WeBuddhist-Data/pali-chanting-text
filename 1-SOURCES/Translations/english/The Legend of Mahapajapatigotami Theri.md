# The Legend of Mahapajapatigotami Theri ^0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-1]]

One day the [Great] Lamp of the World,
the Caravan Leader for men,
dwelt in the Mahāvana Hall,
among Vesali’s gabled roofs. ^1-1

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-2]]

The Victor’s mother’s sister then,
the Buddhist nun Great Gotamī,
was dwelling in a nuns’ refuge,
built in that delightful city.[^f2] ^1-2

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-3]]

This reasoning occurred to her,
thinking [when] she’d gone off alone
from liberated Buddhist nuns
numbering five times one hundred:[^f3] ^1-3

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-4]]
![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-5]]

“I will not be able to see[^f4]
the Buddha’s final nirvana,
[that] of the two chief followers,
nor Rāhul, Ānanda, Nanda. ^1-4

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-6]]

Destroying[^f5] life’s constituents
[and] letting go, I shall go to
nirvana, permitted by [him,]
the Great Sage, the Lord of the World.” ^1-5

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-7]]

[That] reasoning also occurred
to the five hundred Buddhist nuns;
that reasoning also [occurred]
to [nuns] beginning with Khema. ^1-6

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-8]]
![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-9]]

At that time there was an earthquake;
the thunder of the gods did roar.
Weighed down by grief, the goddesses
who lived in that refuge [for nuns,]
piteously weeping [at that,]
shed [their] tears there [in the refuge].[^f6] ^1-7

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-9]]
![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-10]]

[And then] all of[^f7] those Buddhist nuns,
after approaching Gotamī,
placing [their] heads upon [her] feet,
spoke these words [they addressed to her,]: ^1-8

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-10]]
![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-11]]

“Sister, gone off alone, there we
were sprinkled with drops of water.
The unshaking earth is shaking,
the thunder of the gods roaring,
lamentations[^f8] are being heard:[^f9]
what then does this mean, Gotamī?”[^f10] ^1-9

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-12]]

She then told everything [to them,]
just as [she had] reasoned it out.
All of them too told [Gotamī,]
just as [they had] reasoned it out. ^1-10

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-13]]

“If [it’s] desired by you, sister —
nirvana, unsurpassed [and] pure —
we too will all reach nirvana,
with Buddha’s consent, Pious One.[^f11] ^1-11

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-14]]

Along with [you] we have gone forth
from home and from existence too;
along with [you] indeed we’ll go
to nirvana, supreme city.” ^1-12

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-15]]

She said, “what is there to be said
to women who are going out?”[^f12]
[And] then along with all [of them]
she quitted [that] Buddhist nuns’ nest.[^f13] ^1-13

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-16]]

“May the goddesses forgive me,
who are dwelling in [this] refuge;
this will be my final vision
of [this] Buddhist nuns’ residence. ^1-14

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-17]]

I’ll go to unconditionedness,
where [there’s neither] death nor decay,
one doesn’t meet the unpleasant,
nor get cut off from pleasant things.” ^1-15

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-18]]

Hearing those words, not passionless,
[those] heirs of the Well-Gone [Buddha,][^f14]
overcome with grief lamented:
“Alas, we have little merit. ^1-16

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-19]]

Without those women this Buddhist
nuns’ nest [now] has become empty;
the Victor’s heirs [now] are not seen,
as stars [disappear] at daybreak. ^1-17

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-20]]

Gotamī goes to nirvana
along with the five hundred [nuns],
like the Ganges [flows to] the sea,
with five hundred tributaries.”[^f15] ^1-18

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-21]]

The faithful laywomen,[^f16] having
seen her[^f17] going along the road,
coming out from [their] houses [then]
bowing down at [her] feet said this: ^1-19

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-22]]

“Great-fortuned one,[^f18] be satisfied.[^f19]
Nirvana’s not proper for you,
abandoning us, destitute” —
distraught like that those women wailed. ^1-20

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-23]]

In order to dispel their grief,
[Gotamī] spoke [this] honeyed speech:
“Enough with [your] crying, children,
today, which is your time to laugh; ^1-21

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-24]]

I have understood suffering,[^f20]
the cause of suffering’s allayed,
I’ve experienced cessation,
I have cultivated the path. ^1-22

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^a-1]]

The First Recitation Portion[^f21] ^a-1

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-25]]

The Teacher’s been worshipped by me,
[I have] done what the Buddha taught!
The heavy load has been laid down,
the ties to existence removed. ^1-23

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-26]]

The reason for which I went forth
from [my] home into homelessness —
I have [now] achieved that purpose:
destruction of all the fetters. ^1-24

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-27]]

While Buddha and his great Teaching
are still around, nothing lacking —
that’s the time for my nirvana;
do not grieve about me, children. ^1-25

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-28]]

Koṇḍañña,[^f22] Ānanda,[^f23] Nanda,[^f24]
Rāhula,[^f25] the Victor remain;
the Assembly’s cheerful and close,
the conceit of rivals is slain. ^1-26

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-29]]

The Famed One in[^f26] Okkāka’s clan
is Exalted,[^f27] the Death-Crusher;[^f28]
children, isn’t it now the time
[for me] to achieve nirvana? ^1-27

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-30]]

My wish [I’ve had] for very long
is [finally] fulfilled today.
This is the time for drums of joy.
What then with [all these] tears, children? ^1-28

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-31]]

If [you feel] compassion for me,
and if you all appreciate
the great Teaching’s stability,
then strong and fervent you should be. ^1-29

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-32]]

Beseeched by me, the Sambuddha
gave ordination to women.
Therefore as I have shown myself,
you all should follow after him.” ^1-30

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-33]]

Having thus advised [those women,]
placed in front by the Buddhist nuns,
going up to [and] worshipping
the Buddha, [she] said this [to him:] ^1-31

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-34]]

“Well-Gone-One, I am your mother,
and you are my father, Hero;
Lord,[^f29] who Gives the Good Teaching’s Joy,[^f30]
O Gotama, I’m born from[^f31] you. ^1-32

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-35]]

Your body, made of flesh and bones,[^f32]
was reared up by me, Well-Gone-One;
my flawless body, made of Truth,[^f33]
was reared up by you, [Gotama.] ^1-33

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-36]]

I suckled you with mother’s milk
which quenches thirst for a moment.
From you I drank the milk of Truth,[^f34]
peaceful without interruption. ^1-34

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-37]]

Great Sage, you owe no debt to me
for protecting and rearing [you].
To obtain such a son is what
women desiring sons [desire].[^f35] ^1-35

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-38]]

Mothers of kings, like Mandhātā,
are sunk into existence sea.
O son, through you I’ve crossed over
[life,] this ocean of becoming. ^1-36

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-39]]

Women can easily obtain
the name “King’s Mother” or “Chief Queen.”
The name, “Mother of the Buddha”
is the hardest [name] to obtain. ^1-37

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-40]]

O Hero, I’ve obtained that name!
[I got] my wish because of you.
Whether little things or big things,
all of that is fulfilled by me. ^1-38

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-41]]

Having abandoned this body,
I want to [reach] full nirvana.
Give me permission, O Hero,
O Dis-ease-Ender,[^f36] O Leader. ^1-39

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-42]]

Stretch forth your feet, like lilies soft,
which are marked with wheel, goad and flag.
I shall make obeisance to you,
with a [mother’s] love for [her] son.[^f37] ^1-40

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-43]]

Show [me your] physical body;
it resembles a heap of gold.
[One last] good look at your body,
[then] off I go to peace, Leader.” ^1-41

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-44]]

Marked with the thirty-two great marks,
it was adorned in radiance:
the Victor showed her[^f38] [his] body,
a pale sun[^f39] through[^f40] an evening cloud.[^f41] ^1-42

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-45]]

Then she laid [her] head down upon
the soles of [his] feet, marked with wheels,
which were like lotuses in bloom,
[as] brilliant as the dawning sun. ^1-43

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-46]]

“I’m bowing to the Sun for Men,[^f42]
the Banner of the Solar Clan;[^f43]
when I have died for the last time,
I will never[^f44] see you again. ^1-44

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-47]]

Chief of the World, it is believed
that women make every error.[^f45]
If there’s any error in me,
forgive it, Mine of Compassion.[^f46] ^1-45

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-48]]

I begged [you,] over and again,
for ordination of women.
If I was in error in that,
forgive it, O Bull Among Men. ^1-46

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-49]]

O Hero, with your permission,
I instructed the Buddhist nuns.
If [I gave] bad advice in that,
forgive it, Lord of Forgiveness.”[^f47] ^1-47

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-50]]

“What’s not forgiven to forgive
in [one who’s] adorned with virtue?[^f48]
What more am I to say to you
when you’re going to nirvana? ^1-48

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-51]]

Those who are desiring escape from the world
in my pure [and] complete Assembly of monks,
are like the fading crescent moon at daybreak
after having seen the ruin of its grasps.”[^f49][^f50] ^1-49

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-52]]

Like the stars and the moon around Mount Meru,
the other nuns circumambulated [him,]
Chief Victor, [and] after bowing at [his] feet,
they stood there gazing at the [Blessed One’s] face. ^1-50

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-53]]

“Formerly [my] eyes and ears weren’t satisfied
by the vision of you nor hearing your speech.
[But now,] having obtained perfection, my mind
is satisfied by the taste of the Teaching. ^1-51

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-54]]

When you roar forth amidst the crowd,
destroying the sophists’ conceit,
those [there] who are seeing your face,
are fortunate, O Bull of Men.[^f51] ^1-52

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-55]]

Battle-Ender,[^f52] fortunate too,
are they who worship your fine feet,
which have broad heels, extended toes,
and nails [the color of] copper. ^1-53

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-56]]

Fortunate too, O Best of Men,
are those who listen to your words,
imperfection-slaying, friendly,
honey[-sweet] and full of gladness. ^1-54

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-57]]

Fortunate am I, Great Hero,
intent on worshipping[^f53] your feet.
The existential desert crossed,
[I] shine due to the good Teaching.”[^f54] ^1-55

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-58]]

Then the pious one[^f55] explained [her
thoughts][^f56] to the Assembly of monks,
and having worshipped Rāhula,
Ānanda [and] Nanda, she said:[^f57] ^1-56

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-59]]
![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-60]]

“I am weary[^f58] of [my] body,
similar to a serpent’s den,
a sickness-house, heap of dis-ease,[^f59]
pasturing in old age and death,
covered with varied flaws and drool,[^f60]
dependent on others, actionless.[^f61]
Therefore I desire nirvana;
give [me your] permission, children.” ^1-57

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-61]]

Nanda and lucky Rāhula,
who were griefless, without constraint,
wise [and] unshakingly steadfast,
reflected on the way things are: ^1-58

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-62]]

“Woe on greed for conditioned things:
as worthless as banana wood,
same as a deluded mirage,
fleeting and constantly changing. ^1-59

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-63]]

In flux are all conditioned things,
in so far as the Victor’s aunt,
the one who suckled the Buddha,
Gotamī, goes without a trace.”[^f62] ^1-60

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-64]]

Ānanda was then [still] training,
fond of the Victor, [but still] sad.
[Beseeching her] there, shedding tears,
he was wailing piteously: ^1-61

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-65]]

“Gotamī is going, smiling;[^f63]
surely then soon the Buddha too
will be going to nirvana,
like a fire whose fuel has run out.” ^1-62

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-66]]

Gotamī said to Ānanda
who was lamenting in this way:
“O son, keen on serving Buddha,
your wisdom’s deep as is the sea,[^f64] ^1-63

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-67]]

[and so] you really should not mourn,
when the time for smiling has come!
Son, [through] your assistance to me,
I have realized nirvana.[^f65] ^1-64

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-68]]

Being requested by you, dear,
[Buddha] gave us ordination.
[Therefore] do not be distressed, son;
your effort is [now] bearing fruit. ^1-65

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-69]]

That state unseen by the ancients,[^f66]
and likewise by rival teachers,
is known by [Buddhist] young maidens,
when they’re [only] seven years old. ^1-66

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-70]]

[So take] your final look [at me,]
preserver of the Buddha’s word;[^f67]
Son, I am going to that place
where one who’s gone cannot be seen.” ^1-67

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-71]]

Once when he was preaching Dhamma,
the Chief Leader of the World sneezed.
At that time, compassionately,
I spoke well-wishing words [to him:] ^1-68

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-72]]

“Live for a long time, Great Hero!
Remain for an aeon, Great Sage!
For the sake of the entire world,
do not grow old [nor] pass away!” ^1-69

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-73]]

The Buddha then said this to me
who had spoken to him like that:
“Buddhas are not to be worshipped,
as you’re worshipping, Gotamī.” ^1-70

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-74]]

“How then, O One with Omniscience,
should the Thus-Gone-Ones be worshipped?
How should Buddhas not be worshipped?
Being asked, tell [all] that to me.” ^1-71

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-75]]

“See [my] followers, united,
vigorously energetic,
constantly firm [in their] effort —
that is worship of the Buddhas.”[^f68] ^1-72

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-76]]

Then, going [back] to the refuge,
[gone off] alone, I reflected:
“the Lord, who Reached the Three Worlds’ Ends,[^f69]
likes a united retinue. ^1-73

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-77]]

Well then, I’ll reach full nirvana;
let me see no hindrance to that!”
I, contemplating in that way,
after seeing the Seventh Sage, ^1-74

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-78]]

announced to [the Buddha,] the Guide,
the time of my full nirvana.
And then he gave [me] his assent:
“you know the time, O Gotamī.” ^1-75

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-79]]

My defilements are [now] burnt up;
all [new] existence is destroyed.
Like elephants with broken chains,
I am living without constraint. ^1-76

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-80]]

Being in Best Buddha’s presence
was a very good thing for me.
The three knowledges are attained;
[I have] done what the Buddha taught! ^1-77

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-81]]

The four analytical modes,
and these eight deliverances,
six special knowledges mastered,
[I have] done what the Buddha taught! ^1-78

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Mahāpajāpatigotamītherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-82]]

“There are fools who doubt that women
[too] gain dhamma-penetration.[^f70]
