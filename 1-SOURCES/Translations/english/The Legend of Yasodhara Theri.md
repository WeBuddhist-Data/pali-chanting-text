# The Legend of Yasodhara Theri ^0

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-1]]

At one time the Leader of Men
was staying in a mountain cave
in the city, Rajagaha,
[which was] lovely and prosperous.[^f1] ^1-1

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-2]]

This is what was reasoned out [then]
by the nun [named] Yasodharā,
who was dwelling in that city,
inside a lovely convent [there]: ^1-2

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-3]]

"Nanda, Rahula and Bhadda;
likewise the two chief followers;
Suddhodana Maharaja,
and Gotamī Pajāpatī; ^1-3

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-3]]
![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-4]]

the great theras of great renown;
and the therīs with great powers:[^f2]
they’ve gone to peaceful [nirvana],
traceless like the flame of a lamp. ^1-4

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-4]]
![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-5]]

While the World’s Lord still is living,
I’ll travel that peaceful path too.”
And having reasoned [all] that out,
she foresaw the end of her life. ^1-5

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-5]]
![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-6]]

Foreseeing that life’s aggregates
would be destroyed that very day,
she set out from her own ashram,
carrying her robe and her bowl. ^1-6

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-6]]
![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-7]]

Honored by one hundred thousand[^f3]
nuns, [the nun named Yasodharā,]
greatly powerful, greatly wise,
[then] went up to the Sambuddha. ^1-7

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-7]]

Having worshipped the Sambuddha,
at the wheel-marked [soles of his feet],
sitting off to one side [of him,]
she spoke these words to the Teacher: ^1-8

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-8]]

“I’m seventy-eight years old now,
the last of old age has arrived;
I’m reporting to the Great Sage:
I’ve attained [sainthood] in a cave. ^1-9

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-9]]

Old age has ripened for me [now];
verily my life’s a trifle.
Giving all you up I will go:
my refuge is made in myself. ^1-10

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-10]]

In the final days of old age,
death breaks [the body into bits];
today at nighttime, Great Hero,
I shall achieve my nirvana. ^1-11

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-11]]

Where there’s no birth, no growing old,
nor sickness and death, O Great Sage,
I’m going to the [great] city
[which,] unconditioned, has no death. ^1-12

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-12]]

Throughout [this vast] multitude here,[^f4]
[all these] revering[^f5] the Teacher,[^f6]
know that [every] imperfection
is forgiven face-to-face, Sage. ^1-13

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-13]]

Transmigrating in existence,
if I have [ever] disturbed you,[^f7]
I’m announcing it, Great Hero;
please forgive my imperfection. ^1-14

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-14]]

After hearing [that] speech of hers,
the Lord of Sages[^f8] [then] said this:
“What better can I say to you,
when you’re going to nirvana? ^1-15

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-15]]

Now[^f9] display [your] superpowers,
doer of my dispensation;
let doubt in the dispensation
be cut off for all assemblies.”[^f10] ^1-16

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-16]]

Having heard the words of the Sage,
the Buddhist nun, Yasodharā,
worshipping the King of Sages,
[then] spoke this speech to the [Buddha:] ^1-17

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-17]]

"I am Yasodharā, Hero;
in the home I was your chief queen,[^f11]
born in the clan of the Śākyas,
established among the women.[^f12] ^1-18

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-18]]

In your household, O Hero, I
was the leader, the lord of all
of the [women there, who numbered]
one hundred thousand ninety six. ^1-19

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-19]]

All of those women, endowed with
the virtues of beauty and grace,
youthful and well-spoken, revere
me, like people [revere] the gods.[^f13] ^1-20

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-20]]

Leader of a thousand maidens
in the home of the Śākyan Son,
they’re the same in pleasure and pain,[^f14]
like gods in [the garden named] “Joy.” ^1-21

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-21]]

Beyond the essence of desire,
fixed as the essence of beauty,
[they’re] unmatched in terms of beauty,
other than [by] the World’s Leader.” ^1-22

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-22]]

Speaking [words] beginning with these,
having risen into the sky,
Yasodharā displayed diverse
powers,[^f15] with Buddha’s permission.[^f16] ^1-23

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-22]]

Worshipping[^f17] the Sambuddha, she
showed the Teacher superpowers.[^f18]
She displayed great superpowers,
diverse, having various forms.[^f19] ^1-24

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-23]]

Body big as the universe,[^f20]
she [made] the continent[^f21] up north
her head; eastern, western [her] wings;
[and made] India her torso; ^1-25

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-24]]

tail feathers: the southern ocean;
[other] feathers: varied rivers;
[her] eyes were the moon and the sun,
[her] crest was cosmic Mount Meru. ^1-26

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-25]]

[In her] beak, mountain at world’s end,[^f22]
[she carried] a tree[^f23] with its roots.
Coming up to [him,] fanning [him,]
she’s worshipping the World’s Leader. ^1-27

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-26]]

She made herself[^f24] an elephant,
likewise a horse, mountain, ocean,
the moon and the sun, Mount Meru,
and Śakra, [the king of the gods]. ^1-28

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-27]]

She covered the thousand-fold world
with blooming lotus,[^f25] [and then said,]
“I am Yasodharā, Hero;
I worship [your feet], Eyeful One." ^1-29

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-28]]

And making Brahma’s form appear,
she preached the doctrine of merit;[^f26]
"I am Yasodharā, Hero;
I worship your feet, Eyeful One.” ^1-30

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-29]]

I’ve mastered the superpowers
[like] the “divine ear” element.
I’m also a master, Great Sage,
of the knowledge in others’ hearts. ^1-31

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-30]]

I remember [my] former lives;
[my] “divine eye” is purified.
All the defilements are destroyed;
[I] will not be reborn again. ^1-32

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-31]]

In meaning and in the Teaching,
etymology and preaching,
[this] knowledge of mine was produced
in your presence, O Great Hero. ^1-33

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-32]]

[My] meeting with [all] the Buddhas,[^f27]
the World-Lords, was well-seen by you;[^f28]
my extensive service [to them]
was for the sake of you, Great Sage. ^1-34

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-33]]

O Sage, recall the good karma,
which formerly [was done] by me;
[that] merit was heaped up by me
for the sake of you, Great Hero. ^1-35

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-34]]

I kept from misconduct, hindered[^f29]
the [nine] impossible places;[^f30]
I have sacrificed[^f31] life [itself]
for the sake of you, Great Hero. ^1-36

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-35]]

I gave myself to be a wife,
several tens of billions [of times].
I was not distressed about that,
for the sake of you, O Great Sage. ^1-37

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-36]]

I gave myself to do service,
several tens of billions [of times].
I was not distressed about that,
for the sake of you, O Great Sage. ^1-38

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-37]]

I gave myself to [provide] food,
several tens of billions [of times].
I was not distressed about that,
for the sake of you, O Great Sage. ^1-39

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-38]]

I have given you [all my] lives,
several tens of billions [of times].
I’ll liberate [myself] from fear,
giving up my life [once more]. ^1-40

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-39]]

Great Sage, for your sake I do not[^f32]
conceal the things of a woman,
numerous clothes of varied types,
ornaments affixed to[^f33] [my] limbs. ^1-41

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-40]]

Wealth and grain have been given up,
villages and also small towns,
fields and sons and daughters [as well]
have been given up, O Great Sage. ^1-42

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-41]]

Elephants, horses, also cows,
slaves [as well as] servant-women
are given up beyond all count
for the sake of you, O Great Sage. ^1-43

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-42]]

Whatever I am told [to give]
as alms to beggars, I give [that];
I don’t witness[^f34] any distress
from giving the ultimate gift. ^1-44

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-43]]

I have experienced[^f35] dis-ease
of diverse types, beyond all count,
in [this] much-varied existence[^f36]
for the sake of you, Great Hero. ^1-45

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-44]]

Attaining comfort[s,] I don’t thrill;
[I do]n’t get distressed by[^f37] troubles.
Everywhere I remain balanced
for the sake of you, O Great Sage. ^1-46

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-45]]

After experiencing [both]
pleasure [and] pain along the way,
the Great Sage reached Awakening,
the Teaching for which[^f38] Buddha[^f39] strived. ^1-47

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-46]]

By you [and] by me there was much
meeting with the other World-Lords,
[whether you’re] the god Brahmā or[^f40]
Gotama Buddha,[^f41] World’s Leader. ^1-48

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-47]]

I performed a lot of service,
for the sake of you, O Great Sage;
while you sought the Buddha’s Teaching,[^f42]
I was [always] your attendant. ^1-49

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-48]]

One hundred thousand aeons [and]
four incalculable [aeons]
hence, Dīpaṅkara, Great Hero,
the Leader of the World was born. ^1-50

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-49]]

[Some]place in a nearby country,
inviting [him,] the Thus-Gone-One,
happy-minded folks are cleaning
the road [on which] he is coming. ^1-51

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-50]]

At that time there was a brahmin
[known by] the name of Sumedha.
He was making the road ready
for the All-Seer who was coming. ^1-52

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-51]]

At that time I was a maiden,
born in a brahmin [family],
known by the name of Sumittā.
I went up to that gathering. ^1-53

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-52]]

With eight handfuls of blue lotus
for offering[^f43] to the Teacher,
in the midst of [all] the people
I saw that fierce [ascetic] sage.[^f44] ^1-54

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-53]]

Seeing [him,] seated atop bark,[^f45]
surpassing[^f46] [and] captivating,[^f47]
then I thought [like this to myself:]
“[this] life of mine is bearing fruit.” ^1-55

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-54]]

At that time I saw [that] sage’s
effort [which was then] bearing fruit;
due to previous karma, my
heart was pleased in the Sambuddha. ^1-56

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-55]]

Making [my] heart even more pleased,
I said, “O lofty-minded sage,
seeing no other gift [to give,]
I’m giving flowers to you, sage.[^f48] ^1-57

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-56]]

There are five handfuls for you, sage;
the [remaining] three[^f49] are for me.
Let there be success through[^f50] this [gift]
for your Awakening,[^f51] O sage.” ^1-58

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^a-1]]

The Fourth Recitation Portion ^a-1

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-57]]

Famed One, Great Sage[^f53] who was coming. ^1-59

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-58]]

![[1-SOURCES/Text/Yasodharātherīapadānaṃ.md#^1-60]]
